# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two audiences. Primary: the league players (roughly 5-15 people) who are checking their own stats, standings, and race recaps after each session. They care about their position, their rivals, and the narrative of the season. Secondary: casual spectators, friends and family who follow the league without racing. They want to understand what happened, who's winning, and why it matters.

Both audiences are on mobile as often as desktop. Neither needs onboarding; they already know the context. They open the site to find a specific answer fast, or to browse recent coverage.

## Product Purpose

CGR League is a private F1-style racing league statistics platform. It stores multi-season race results, driver and team entries, and track data, and surfaces them as standings, race results, driver profiles, and AI-generated articles. It makes a small private racing league feel real and worth caring about. Success means the site feels like a legitimate source of record for the league, not a spreadsheet replacement.

## Brand Personality

Credible, precise, alive. The tone is serious enough to be taken as a real stats platform, with enough personality to reflect that it's tracking real people and real rivalries. No corporate distance. No tabloid noise.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dark SaaS**: the blue-accent, card-grid, opacity-token dashboard aesthetic of 2020-2024. Looks like every B2B tool. This league deserves its own visual identity.
- **Official F1 website**: overly corporate, marketing-heavy, treats stats as secondary to brand promotion. The stats are the product here.

## Design Principles

1. **The data is the hero.** UI chrome stays quiet. Standings, results, and driver stats command the page. Decoration that competes with data loses.
2. **Specific over generic.** Every design decision should feel like it was made for this league, not for "motorsport" as a category. Use the real drivers, real rivalries, real team colors where they're known.
3. **Credibility through density.** A data-rich layout signals seriousness. Sparse, bloated layouts feel unfinished. Pack information with precision; don't pad with whitespace.
4. **Precision over flourish.** Typography and spacing carry the visual weight. Decoration (gradients, glows, glass) only earns its place when it serves a functional role.
5. **Fast to the answer.** The primary interaction is finding something specific: "where am I in the standings," "who won last race," "how do I compare to my rival." Navigation and layout must never slow that down.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the baseline. Standard contrast ratios (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text) and keyboard navigation throughout. No special accommodations identified for this audience, but reduced-motion preferences should be respected for any animations.
